Vigo is a municipality and city in the province of Pontevedra, in the autonomous community of Galicia, Spain. With a population of 294,489 inhabitants (INE 2025), it is the most populous municipality in Galicia and the fourteenth in Spain, as well as the largest city in the country without provincial capital status. Additionally, it constitutes the most densely populated urban area of the conurbation that stretches over 300 km of the Rías Baixas and is the central core of a metropolitan area that includes 13 other municipalities, with a total of 483,468 registered inhabitants. According to the Statistical Atlas of Urban Areas in Spain, published by the Ministry of Transport and Sustainable Mobility, the Vigo-Pontevedra functional urban area forms the twelfth metropolitan area in the country. Geographically, Vigo is located in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula, about 33 km north of the border with Portugal. Its municipal area covers 109.06 km², with a population density of 2,700.2 inhabitants/km².

The economy of Vigo is characterized by high diversification. Its main drivers are the automotive industry, shipbuilding, industrial fishing, and activities linked to the free trade zone and the port, which also serves as the base port for the Spanish oceanographic fleet. Other important sectors include commerce, services, and tourism, boosted by its beaches, cultural offerings, festivals and local celebrations, the Camino de Santiago on its Portuguese coastal route, and more recently, the city’s Christmas lighting displays.

The city hosts various institutions and organizations, among which stand out Afundación, the European Fisheries Control Agency, the Vigo Free Trade Zone Consortium, the Vigo Port Fishing Shipowners’ Cooperative, the Camões Institute, the headquarters of the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park, and the University of Vigo. This concentration of companies and institutions results in nearly 500,000 people living, working, or studying in Vigo daily, representing approximately 53% of the province’s population and 18.46% of Galicia’s population. The asteroid (127870) Vigo was named in honor of the city.

Toponymy

The origin of the toponym "Vigo" is subject to various theories. The most widely accepted holds that it derives from the Latin term Vicus, meaning village or settlement, in reference to the Roman nucleus of Vico Spacorum. Other hypotheses identify it with the Roman city of Burbida Magna or suggest an origin in the Viking word Úig, meaning bay. However, none of these theories has consensus among historians.

Symbols

Coat of Arms

The current coat of arms derives from the old one that adorned the Neptune Fountain, located at Puerta del Sol. It represents a square castle with a tower, accompanied by the olive tree that stood in the atrium of the Concatedral de Santa María, all on an island in a sea of five wavy stripes, symbolizing the city’s maritime character.

Flag

The flag of Vigo is believed to have been used during the Reconquista and the Spanish War of Independence. In the 1930s, it represented the maritime province of Vigo, and on April 7, 1987, the city council officially adopted it. It consists of two colors, white and red, arranged in a sotuer pattern: white on the sides and red on the top and bottom.
